Hidden Places - Relaxation in Nature

Designing a website guide with three hidden places of my choice

Year: 2025

Software: Figma, HTML, CSS, JavaScript

Click to view Figma prototype Click to view Coded version Click to watch video presentation of the project

The goal of this project was to introduce three of my favourite places in Aarhus to someone who would enjoy them. I chose relaxation in nature as the topic of my project and decided to invite the users on a journey to Bellevue Beach, Marselisborg Palace Gardens and Brabrand Lake. I designed the website so it’s easy to navigate and looks similar to other traveling websites. The subpages are meant to resemble a whiteboard with notes about the place, and some photos too.

First, I wanted the user to see a big hero image that tells the vibe of the project - relax and nature.

Then, I wanted the user to see a simple screen with all the necessary info about each place and some small interesting details like songs connected to the place or what can be found there.

The whole project uses photos taken by me.

Blå Sol

Blå Sol Festival

Expanding the existing festival app by extra functionalities - group project

Year: 2026

Software: Figma, HTML, CSS, JavaScript

Click to view Figma prototype Click to view Coded version Click to view GitHub Click to view FigJam

For this project, my group decided to add extra functionalities to the already existing Blå Sol festival app. We worked on designing an interactive map where people can not only find their way around the festival grounds more easily but also share their location with friends. That addition helps festival goers enjoy their time at the event without worrying about getting lost.
My main input into the project was coming up with a shared group schedule design, and bringing the idea of the 404 page. I also added some small details like starting the project with a phone lock screen to keep the project presentation even more real and mobile-like.

The schedule page works in the way that each app user creates their profile and connects with their friends. Then after liking artists on the schedule page, users get a group schedule generated based on who was liked by their friends. That way, festival goers get a quick overview of the concerts, which they can use during the festival.

For the starting page, since Blå Sol festival is based in Randers, I wanted the project to have this realistic touch of going through the phone of someone from the city. Therefore, I made the lockscreen to be the logo of the football team from Randers.

Lastly, I would like to show some mockups of the most important part of the project, which was the map. On the map, users can see their friends and their locations. We use the real map of the festival to make it more accurate and easy to find their way back to each other in case they get separated. My group decided to use that kind of a map, since festivals often happen on fields, which are not that accurate on Google or Apple Maps.

Cat Bell

Cat Bell

Quick few days project where I got to use my imagination and create a product concept

Year: 2026

Software: Figma

Click to view Figma prototype Click to watch video presentation of the project

This project was a homework task from the design course. I was supposed to come up with a digital solution that would help with an everyday problem or make something easier. I immediately thought about my cat and how I never know when she comes back home and waits for me to let her inside. That's how I created Cat Bell.

First, I designed the logo by mixing a cat icon with a camera icon. I added the green color which I sampled from my cat's eye colour in one of her photos.

Then I made a simple camera design in the same green color and put the logo on it.

Later, I created an image that resembles a camera scanning my cat's face, just to visualise how the whole thing would work.

And lastly, I created some Figma mockups showing how the app could look. They start with the notification design and move on to opening the app for the first time and then adding your cats.

As mentioned before, this project was just a quick homework task, but I wanted to include it in my portfolio because I think it really shows my creativity!

About me!

Hi! Welcome to my portfolio!

My name is Magdalena Szymanska, I’m 26 years old and originally from Poland. I’m currently in my second year of the Multimedia Design programme at Business Academy Aarhus.

My journey with design started when I was a kid and my parents got their first computer. My dad would draw simple houses in Microsoft Paint, and I could spend hours colouring them. As I got older, I started creating my own digital paintings and PowerPoint presentations about literally everything.

My curiosity for visual design continued into my teenage years. I loved photographing anything I found beautiful or interesting— from sunsets to buildings in places I visited. I also started experimenting with Photoshop and Canva, slowly discovering how much I enjoyed creating and working with visuals.

Another important part of my story is Denmark. When I was around 10, my aunt moved here, and my parents started bringing me to Denmark every summer. From then on, I knew that one day I wanted to live and study here. I started working towards that goal when I was 14, taking extra English classes and preparing for the language tests I would eventually need to apply to schools in Denmark.

When the time came to choose my education, I decided to study Marketing Management at IBA in Kolding. At the time, Multimedia Design felt a little intimidating, so I chose a path that felt more familiar.

After moving to Denmark, I also started learning Danish. I’m still working on the speaking part, but I’m slowly getting there!

A few years after graduating, I decided it was time to take the risk and return to what I had always been drawn to: design.

And now, here I am—halfway through my Multimedia Design journey, combining my background in marketing with my passion for visual communication, creativity, and digital design.

I’m excited to see where this journey takes me next!

How I think about design

I want my designs to be visually interesting, but they should also have a purpose. I like thinking about who will use something, what they need, and how I can make the experience simple and understandable.

I enjoy combining creativity with strategy. Instead of only thinking about how something looks, I try to understand the problem first and then find a solution that makes sense for the people experiencing it.

A book that influences my designs

Don't Make Me Think — Steve Krug

I was introduced to Don't Make Me Think by Steve Krug during my Marketing Management studies, and it is a book that has stayed with me.

One of the main ideas I take from it is that good design should feel intuitive. When I work on my own designs, I try to put myself in the user's position and think about whether something is clear, understandable, and easy to navigate.

It reminds me not to overcomplicate things just because I can. I want my designs to look good, but I also want people to understand them without having to stop and figure everything out.

What matters to me

For me, good design is about finding a balance between creativity, strategy, and the people who will actually use what I create.

I am still learning and developing my skills, but I genuinely enjoy the process of improving, experimenting, receiving feedback, and seeing an idea turn into something real.
